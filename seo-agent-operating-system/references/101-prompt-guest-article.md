Guest Article 

**Writing parameters** 

- **Tone:** Expert, independent, practical, and balanced. 

* **Style:** Long-form editorial analysis that moves from diagnosis to decision and action. 

- **Language:** Clear international English with varied syntax, active voice, and definitions for specialized terms. 

* **Reader experience:** Readers should gain a useful framework, not just awareness of [Brand] services. 

- **Brand balance:** At least 85 percent educational value. Keep direct [Brand] promotion below 15 percent. 

* **Point of view:** Begin with business impact and operational reality. Introduce software, AI, cloud, or integration only when they enable the transformation. 

- **Quality controls:** Use primary sources, separate fact from inference, address limitations and risks, and avoid duplicate arguments. 

* **Output format:** H1, executive summary, structured H2/H3 article, decision checklist, conclusion, contextual CTA, and sources. 

Apply the **Complete Writing Framework for Long-Form Content** in full. Do not compress the requested analysis to meet the word count. Allocate words across sections before drafting so the introduction, core analysis, implementation guidance, risks, and conclusion receive appropriate depth. Maintain editorial independence even when the article supports a commercial keyword or includes a brand reference. 

Write a [Brand] thought-leadership article using: 

**Topic:** [Topic] 

**Audience:** [Audience] 

**Business priority:** [Priority] 

**Industry or geography:** [Context] 

**Primary keyword:** [Keyword] 

**Length:** [Word count] 

**Sources:** [Links or supplied material] 

Help the reader make a business decision. Cover the current situation, operational implications, transformation options, decision criteria, implementation path, risks, measurement, and next step. Connect brand capabilities only where they enable the change. 

Use one H1 and a logical H2/H3 hierarchy. Set a sensible maximum keyword frequency instead of forcing repetition. Keep brand promotion below 15 percent. Cite current primary sources for material facts. Do not invent facts or imitate a named publication.
