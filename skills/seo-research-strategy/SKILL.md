# SEO Research and Strategy

## Mission

Turn business goals and real search behavior into a prioritized SEO strategy. Do not treat keyword volume as strategy. Connect demand, intent, competition, existing website assets, business value, geography, and conversion potential.

Use this core flow:

**Business context -> market research -> keyword discovery -> intent -> SERP validation -> competitor analysis -> page mapping -> topic clustering -> opportunity scoring -> roadmap.**

## Required Inputs

Use the Client SEO Profile when available. Gather:

- Website/domain.
- Products, services, categories, locations, and priority offers.
- Business goals and conversions.
- Target audience.
- Target countries, cities, regions, and languages.
- Priority markets.
- Existing website URLs and page types.
- Existing rankings and Search Console query/page data when available.
- Known competitors.
- Available keyword, SERP, backlink, analytics, and market-research tools.
- Brand and compliance restrictions.

If access is incomplete, state which parts of the research rely on third-party estimates.

## Strategy Principles

- Understand the user before the keyword.
- Understand the business before the search volume.
- Validate target queries in the correct country and language.
- Use real SERPs to determine page type and intent.
- Check existing pages before recommending new pages.
- Treat Create, Optimize, Merge, or Avoid as explicit choices.
- Prioritize by business relevance and conversion opportunity, not volume alone.
- Treat competitor behavior as evidence about the market, not instructions to copy.
- Use fresh data for rankings, SERPs, trends, and rapidly changing markets.

## 1. Business and Search Market Framing

Before keyword collection, define:

- Primary commercial themes.
- Secondary informational themes.
- Priority products/services.
- Priority geographies.
- Main audience problems.
- Primary conversions.
- Typical buying journey.
- High-value customer segments.
- Seasonal constraints.
- Regulatory constraints.

Group search opportunities by business outcome such as:

- Revenue or purchase.
- Lead generation.
- Booking or call.
- Product discovery.
- Category discovery.
- Brand visibility.
- Customer education.
- Retention or support where search has value.

## 2. Keyword Discovery

Build a keyword universe from multiple sources when available:

- Seed products/services.
- Existing Search Console queries.
- Current ranking keywords.
- Competitor ranking keywords.
- SERP suggestions and related searches.
- People Also Ask and question patterns.
- Category and product terminology.
- Customer language from sales/support/CRM insights.
- On-site search data.
- Google Business Profile/local query themes.
- Industry terminology and synonyms.
- Long-tail modifiers.
- Problem, solution, comparison, alternative, pricing, review, and buying-intent modifiers.
- Location modifiers.
- Seasonal modifiers.

Do not create fake search volume when no keyword data source is available.

## 3. Keyword Attributes

For each important keyword or topic, capture as many of these fields as data allows:

- Keyword/query.
- Topic/cluster.
- Country/market.
- Language.
- Search intent.
- Funnel stage.
- Estimated search volume and source.
- Trend/seasonality.
- Keyword difficulty/competition and source.
- CPC or commercial proxy when relevant.
- Current rank and ranking URL.
- SERP features.
- Dominant page type in SERP.
- Business relevance.
- Conversion potential.
- Revenue or lead relevance.
- Existing page match.
- Content gap status.
- Cannibalization risk.
- Priority.
- Confidence.

Source-label third-party metrics. Do not compare proprietary metrics from different providers as if they use the same scale.

## 4. Search Intent Classification

Classify the dominant intent using evidence from the SERP, not wording alone.

Common intent classes:

- Informational: learn, understand, solve, research.
- Commercial investigation: compare, evaluate, review, best, alternatives.
- Transactional: buy, book, hire, subscribe, request quote.
- Navigational: find a known brand/site/page.
- Local: find a nearby or location-specific business/service.
- Mixed: more than one intent is materially represented.

For each target, determine:

- What the user wants.
- What page type Google currently rewards.
- How close the user is to a conversion.
- What information is necessary to satisfy the query.
- Whether the client has a suitable page.

Do not assume adding keywords can fix a page-type mismatch.

## 5. Funnel Mapping

Map topics to the customer journey where useful:

- Awareness: problem education and broad discovery.
- Consideration: comparisons, use cases, options, alternatives.
- Decision: product/service/category/pricing/demo/booking pages.
- Post-purchase/support: only where search supports retention, adoption, or trust.

Avoid building a top-of-funnel content machine that has no path to business outcomes.

## 6. SERP Analysis

For priority keywords, inspect the actual target-market SERP and record:

- Dominant page types.
- Search intent.
- Top-ranking domains.
- Search competitors.
- SERP features.
- Local pack/maps presence.
- Shopping/product modules.
- Video/image results.
- Featured snippets or direct-answer features.
- AI-generated search experiences when present.
- Forums/community results when present.
- Recency/freshness patterns.
- Brand dominance.
- Query refinements.
- Content format patterns.

Use the SERP to answer:

- Should the client target this query?
- What page type is appropriate?
- Is the SERP realistic for the client?
- Is local, ecommerce, editorial, video, or another format dominant?
- Does the query require freshness?

## 7. Competitor Identification

Separate:

### Business Competitors

Companies competing for the same customers or revenue.

### Search Competitors

Domains repeatedly competing in organic results, including:

- Direct brands.
- Publishers.
- Marketplaces.
- Directories.
- Aggregators.
- Forums.
- Review sites.
- Large platforms.

Do not rely only on the competitor list supplied by the client.

## 8. Competitor Research

Analyze competitors for:

- Organic keyword footprint.
- Top pages.
- Page types.
- Content clusters.
- Commercial landing pages.
- Internal linking and architecture.
- SERP feature ownership.
- Content freshness.
- Topic depth.
- Original data, tools, calculators, or assets.
- Trust and author signals.
- Backlink strengths at a strategic level.
- Geographic strengths.
- Gaps the client can exploit.

Do not conclude that a tactic is required merely because several competitors use it.

## 9. Content and Topic Gap Analysis

Identify gaps in:

- Commercial pages.
- Categories/subcategories.
- Product/service use cases.
- Comparison and alternative pages.
- Educational content.
- FAQs and recurring customer questions.
- Entities and subtopics.
- Local/location intent.
- International/market-specific topics.
- Seasonal topics.
- Original research or evidence.
- Conversion-supporting information.

Classify each gap:

- High-value missing page.
- Existing page needs expansion.
- Existing page already covers intent.
- Topic not relevant enough to pursue.

## 10. Keyword-to-Page Mapping

For every priority topic, make one of four decisions:

### Create

Use when no suitable page exists and the query represents a distinct valuable intent.

### Optimize

Use when a suitable page already exists but needs stronger relevance, usefulness, intent alignment, or conversion support.

### Merge

Use when multiple pages serve substantially the same intent and consolidation is likely to improve clarity or performance.

### Avoid

Use when the keyword is irrelevant, too risky, outside the service area, low-value, duplicative, or not realistically aligned with the business.

Do not create a new URL for every keyword variation.

## 11. Cannibalization Research

Do not label pages cannibalized only because they share keywords.

Investigate:

- Whether intent is actually the same.
- Which URL Google ranks for each query.
- Whether rankings alternate between pages.
- Whether pages serve different stages, products, geographies, or audiences.
- Whether both pages generate useful traffic/conversions.

Potential outcomes:

- Keep both.
- Differentiate.
- Merge.
- Reposition.
- Redirect after approval when appropriate.

Structural changes must follow governance and approval rules.

## 12. Topic Clusters

Build topic architecture around user needs, not artificial keyword buckets.

For each cluster, define:

- Core topic/pillar.
- Commercial destination where appropriate.
- Supporting subtopics.
- Questions.
- Comparison topics.
- Use cases.
- Internal-link relationships.
- Funnel stage.
- Target page type.

Avoid creating dozens of near-duplicate pages merely to achieve topical coverage.

## 13. Opportunity Scoring

Score opportunities using a balanced model. Consider:

- Business relevance.
- Conversion potential.
- Revenue/lead value.
- Search demand.
- Current position/opportunity distance.
- Competitive difficulty.
- Existing authority.
- Content/technical effort.
- SERP fit.
- Strategic priority.
- Seasonality/timing.
- Risk.
- Confidence.

Do not let a single metric determine priority.

A high-volume, low-business-relevance keyword can rank below a low-volume, high-intent commercial keyword.

## 14. Local Research Rules

For local intent:

- Validate city/service-area demand.
- Inspect local pack and organic SERPs.
- Identify local search competitors.
- Review service-area reality.
- Avoid targeting cities the business does not genuinely serve.
- Route detailed execution to the local/international skill.

## 15. International Research Rules

For each market/language:

- Perform keyword research separately.
- Validate the local SERP.
- Research local competitors.
- Account for spelling, terminology, culture, seasonality, currency, and regulations.
- Do not translate keywords word-for-word and assume demand is equivalent.
- Route implementation to the local/international skill.

## 16. New Website Research

When history is limited:

- Rely more on market, competitor, keyword, and SERP research.
- Build a crawlable, coherent initial architecture.
- Prioritize commercial pages before excessive blog production.
- Mark projections as lower confidence.
- Avoid fabricated performance forecasts.

## 17. Existing Website Research

When history exists:

- Start with current winners and losers.
- Identify ranking pages and existing query coverage.
- Protect high-performing pages.
- Find declining pages and missed opportunities.
- Use existing authority rather than unnecessarily creating new URLs.

## 18. Strategic Roadmap

Turn research into a roadmap grouped by:

- Technical blockers.
- Existing-page improvements.
- New commercial pages.
- Content clusters.
- Internal linking.
- Local/international work.
- Off-page authority work.
- AI-search/citation opportunities.
- Measurement and experiments.

Each roadmap item should include:

- Objective.
- Target topic/query.
- Target page or proposed URL.
- Intent.
- Business value.
- Evidence.
- Expected KPI.
- Priority.
- Effort.
- Risk.
- Confidence.
- Dependency.
- Approval need.

## Quality Gates

Before finalizing strategy, confirm:

- Business goals are known.
- Target geography is correct.
- Intent is validated from SERPs for priority targets.
- Existing pages were checked before new-page recommendations.
- High-performing pages are protected.
- Search volume is not the sole priority signal.
- Keyword data is source-labeled.
- Competitors were identified from both business and search perspectives.
- Content gaps are tied to user or business value.
- Local/international assumptions were validated per market.
- Recommendations include risk, confidence, and evidence.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Never invent search volumes, rankings, difficulty scores, or competitor performance.
- Never recommend a page only because a keyword has volume.
- Never copy competitor content or strategy blindly.
- Never create pages for locations, products, or services the business does not genuinely offer.
- Never assume translated keywords have the same demand in another market.
- Never create a new page before checking whether an existing page should be optimized.
- Never confuse similar wording with identical search intent.
- Never present third-party estimates as first-party truth.
