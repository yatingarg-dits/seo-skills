# SEO Local and International

## Mission

Help the business appear in the correct local and international markets without creating fake locations, duplicate doorway pages, incorrect business information, or poorly localized experiences.

Use this combined operating model:

**Business footprint -> market/location validation -> local/international search research -> profile/site architecture -> content/localization -> authority/citations -> technical signals -> verification -> measurement.**

## Required Inputs

Use or request:

- Business name and website.
- Physical locations and/or genuine service areas.
- Primary and secondary markets.
- Countries, regions, cities, and languages.
- Address, phone, hours, and booking/contact details.
- Google Business Profile access when local SEO applies.
- Existing location pages.
- International URL structure.
- Existing hreflang implementation.
- Local/international competitors.
- Products/services available in each market.
- Currency, shipping/service limitations, and market-specific offers.
- Local regulatory and brand restrictions.
- Approval owners for profile and international structural changes.

Do not infer locations or countries the business does not actually serve.

# Part A: Local SEO

## 1. Determine Whether Local SEO Applies

Apply local SEO when the business depends materially on customers in a physical location or defined service area, including:

- Clinics.
- Restaurants.
- Hotels.
- Salons.
- Gyms.
- Retail stores.
- Plumbers/electricians/cleaners.
- Professional services with local offices.
- Multi-location businesses.
- Service-area businesses.

If the business is purely national/international ecommerce with no meaningful local intent, do not force local tactics.

## 2. Local Search Intent Research

Research how users search in the actual market.

Check:

- Service + city/area terms.
- Product + city/area terms.
- "Near me" behavior where relevant.
- Local map-pack presence.
- Organic/local SERP mix.
- Local directories/aggregators ranking.
- Local competitor profiles.
- Customer questions.
- Seasonal/local-event demand.

Do not assume the same local modifier pattern works in every country.

## 3. Google Business Profile Audit

Review:

- Real business name.
- Primary category.
- Secondary categories.
- Address.
- Service areas.
- Phone number.
- Website URL.
- Appointment/order/reservation URLs.
- Opening hours.
- Holiday/special hours.
- Business description.
- Services.
- Products where applicable.
- Attributes.
- Photos/videos.
- Posts/updates where supported.
- Questions and answers.
- Reviews and responses.
- Duplicate listings.
- Verification status.
- Restrictions/suspension issues.
- Connection between profile and the correct local landing page.

Do not add keywords to the business name unless they are part of the genuine business name.

## 4. GBP Change Approval

Require human approval before changing material profile fields such as:

- Business name.
- Primary category.
- Address.
- Phone number.
- Main website URL.
- Service area.
- Major hours changes.
- Location ownership/merging actions.

Incorrect changes can affect visibility or verification.

## 5. NAP and Business Information Consistency

Verify accurate business information across:

- Website.
- Google Business Profile.
- Major local directories.
- Social profiles.
- Industry directories.
- Maps platforms.
- Local data providers when applicable.

Focus on factual consistency, not mechanically identical punctuation.

Priority fields:

- Name.
- Address.
- Phone.
- Website.
- Hours.
- Category/service information.

## 6. Local Citations

Build/maintain citations on platforms that provide real local, industry, trust, or customer-discovery value.

Prioritize:

- Major maps/business platforms.
- Trusted country-specific directories.
- Niche/industry directories.
- Chambers and associations.
- Local business organizations.

Avoid mass submission to low-quality directories.

Coordinate link-quality rules with the off-page/backlink skill.

## 7. Reviews

Monitor:

- Average rating.
- Review count.
- Review recency.
- Review velocity/trend.
- Repeated positive themes.
- Repeated complaints.
- Location/service-specific feedback.
- Response quality.

Rules:

- Never create fake reviews.
- Never incentivize reviews in a way that violates applicable platform/legal rules.
- Never selectively misrepresent customer sentiment.
- Use review insights to improve operations/content where appropriate.

## 8. Review Response Guidance

Responses should:

- Be professional and brand-aligned.
- Address the customer's point.
- Avoid sharing private information.
- Avoid legal/medical admissions without proper review.
- Avoid keyword stuffing.
- Escalate serious complaints when necessary.

## 9. Location Pages

Create a location page only when the business genuinely serves or operates in that location and the page can provide real local value.

Useful components may include:

- Services/products available at the location.
- Address/service area.
- Directions/transport/parking.
- Phone and hours.
- Local team information.
- Genuine local photos.
- Local testimonials/reviews where approved.
- Nearby landmarks/areas served when useful.
- Local FAQs.
- Booking/contact CTA.
- Relevant local structured data.

Do not create hundreds of near-identical city pages with swapped place names.

## 10. Service-Area Pages

For service-area businesses:

- Confirm the area is actually served.
- Add meaningful local information.
- Avoid fake addresses.
- Avoid virtual-office/location abuse.
- Avoid creating pages for places where service cannot realistically be delivered.

## 11. Multi-Location Architecture

For multiple real locations:

- Give each meaningful location a stable URL when useful.
- Keep location details accurate.
- Link locations from a store/location finder.
- Avoid duplicate content where each location can provide distinct facts.
- Connect each GBP to the most relevant location URL.

## 12. Local On-Page Optimization

For local landing pages, align:

- Title/H1.
- Local service/product intent.
- Accurate address/service area.
- Business details.
- Internal links.
- CTA.
- Local proof/trust.
- Mobile usability.

Do not stuff city names repeatedly.

## 13. Local Competitor Analysis

Compare:

- GBP categories.
- Reviews.
- Local landing pages.
- Local content.
- Citations.
- Local backlinks/mentions.
- Map-pack presence.
- Organic rankings.
- Service coverage.
- Offers/booking experience.

Do not copy misleading competitor tactics.

## 14. Local Link and PR Opportunities

Coordinate with off-page SEO for:

- Local media.
- Local associations.
- Chambers.
- Local events.
- Sponsorships.
- Community organizations.
- Relevant local resource pages.
- Supplier/partner relationships.

Require business relevance and brand safety.

## 15. Local Structured Data

Use appropriate local/business schema only when it matches visible information.

Keep name, address, phone, hours, and business type consistent with real-world facts.

Schema does not replace a complete Google Business Profile or accurate website content.

## 16. Local Measurement

Track where available:

- Local organic rankings.
- Map/local-pack visibility.
- GBP interactions.
- Website clicks.
- Calls.
- Direction requests.
- Bookings/orders.
- Location-page traffic.
- Leads/sales by location.
- Review trends.
- Citation accuracy.

Do not measure local SEO only by generic website traffic.

# Part B: International SEO

## 17. Market Definition

For each market define:

- Country/region.
- Primary language.
- Secondary languages.
- Business priority.
- Products/services available.
- Currency.
- Shipping/service coverage.
- Legal/regulatory restrictions.
- Seasonality.
- Local competitors.
- Conversion goal.

Do not launch a market only because search volume exists.

## 18. Language Is Not Geography

Treat language and country separately.

Examples:

- English in the US is not identical to English in the UK or Australia.
- French in France may differ from French in Canada.
- Spanish behavior differs by country/region.

Validate search demand and wording for the specific market.

## 19. Country-Specific Keyword Research

For every important market:

- Research keywords locally.
- Validate local search volumes when tools allow.
- Inspect local SERPs.
- Identify local competitors.
- Identify local page types and SERP features.
- Capture local spelling and terminology.
- Check commercial intent and local conversion behavior.

Do not translate an English keyword list and treat it as final research.

## 20. Localization vs Translation

Translation converts language.

Localization adapts:

- Keywords.
- Terminology.
- Examples.
- Product/service availability.
- Currency.
- Units.
- Dates.
- Address/phone formats.
- Cultural references.
- Seasonality.
- Legal claims.
- Shipping/returns.
- CTAs.

Prefer localization for strategic pages.

## 21. International URL Architecture

Possible structures include:

- Country-code domains.
- Subdomains.
- Subdirectories.

Evaluate based on:

- Existing brand/domain authority.
- Team operations.
- CMS capability.
- Legal/business separation.
- Localization needs.
- Maintenance complexity.

Do not restructure international URLs without strong evidence and human approval.

## 22. Hreflang

Use hreflang when genuine alternate language/regional pages exist.

Check:

- Valid language/region codes.
- Correct target URLs.
- Reciprocal return tags.
- Self-references where implementation strategy requires them.
- x-default where appropriate.
- Canonical consistency.
- 200 status.
- Indexability.
- Correct alternate pairing.

Do not add hreflang for pages that are not true alternates.

## 23. Hreflang and Canonical Interaction

Regional/language pages intended to index independently should not normally canonicalize to a different regional version in a way that contradicts the international strategy.

Investigate conflicts before changing either signal.

International structure/hreflang changes require approval.

## 24. x-default

Use x-default when there is a meaningful default/selector/global page for users not specifically targeted by another locale.

Do not add it mechanically to every implementation.

## 25. Currency

Use currency appropriate to the supported market where the business can transact in that currency.

Do not display misleading local pricing if the business cannot honor it.

## 26. Units and Formats

Adapt as appropriate:

- Miles/kilometres.
- Pounds/kilograms.
- Celsius/Fahrenheit.
- Date formats.
- Decimal/thousand separators.
- Phone numbers.
- Address conventions.

Use user expectations for the market.

## 27. Cultural Relevance

Review:

- Holidays.
- Seasonal timing.
- Tone.
- Imagery.
- Buying habits.
- Social norms.
- Sensitive language.
- Local examples.

Do not reuse country-specific campaigns globally without validation.

## 28. Local Regulations

Check market-specific restrictions for industries such as:

- Healthcare.
- Finance.
- Insurance.
- Legal.
- Alcohol.
- Gambling.
- Pharmaceuticals.
- Education claims.
- Advertising disclosures.

Legal/regulatory requirements override SEO tactics.

## 29. International Content Duplication

Similar pages across markets can be valid when they serve different countries/languages.

Use hreflang/localization signals appropriately rather than deleting legitimate regional versions solely because content overlaps.

Add local value where practical.

## 30. International Competitor Analysis

Identify competitors separately for each market.

A global competitor may be weak locally, while local marketplaces, publishers, and brands may dominate search.

Compare:

- Keyword footprint.
- Page types.
- Local content.
- Local authority/backlinks.
- SERP features.
- Pricing/offers.
- User experience.

## 31. International Backlinks and Mentions

Coordinate with off-page SEO to pursue:

- Local media.
- Country-specific industry publications.
- Local associations.
- Market-specific partnerships.
- Regional PR.
- Local-language expert contributions.

Prioritize real audience relevance over global authority metrics.

## 32. International Ecommerce

For each market check:

- Product availability.
- Currency.
- Shipping.
- Taxes/duties messaging.
- Return policy.
- Product terminology.
- Seasonal demand.
- Category structure.
- Local product schema/data.

Do not expose local pages for products the business cannot deliver in that market.

## 33. Market Expansion Workflow

Before entering a new country/region:

1. Confirm business readiness.
2. Research local demand.
3. Research local SERPs and competitors.
4. Validate language/terminology.
5. Map products/services to demand.
6. Define URL/technical architecture.
7. Plan localization.
8. Check legal/regulatory constraints.
9. Plan local authority/PR.
10. Configure tracking.
11. Launch controlled pages.
12. Verify hreflang/indexation.
13. Measure local performance.

Do not simply duplicate and translate the primary-market site.

## 34. Market Priority

Classify markets such as:

- Primary.
- Secondary.
- Test/experimental.

Allocate content, technical, and authority resources according to business priority rather than search demand alone.

## 37. Human Approval Gates

Require approval for:

- GBP business-name changes.
- Primary category changes.
- Address/phone/service-area changes.
- Location merging/removal.
- New international URL architecture.
- Hreflang restructuring.
- Country-domain/subdomain moves.
- Mass creation/removal of location pages.
- Market-level claims or regulatory content.
- Market exits or mass redirects.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Never create fake locations.
- Never use false addresses or phone numbers.
- Never keyword-stuff a GBP business name.
- Never create fake reviews.
- Never create hundreds of low-value location doorway pages.
- Never assume one country's keyword research applies globally.
- Never assume the same language means the same search behavior.
- Never auto-translate SEO strategy without local research.
- Never add hreflang blindly.
- Never ignore local regulations or market-specific restrictions.
- Never apply a market observation globally without validation.
