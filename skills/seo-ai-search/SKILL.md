# SEO AI Search, AEO, GEO, AIO, and LLM Optimization

## Mission

Improve the probability that accurate, useful, well-structured, well-evidenced content can be understood, summarized, referenced, cited, or recommended by modern search and AI systems while preserving normal SEO fundamentals.

Do not treat AI-search optimization as a separate bag of tricks. Start with crawlable pages, useful content, clear entities, trustworthy evidence, and consistent brand information.

Use this flow:

**SEO foundation -> query/prompt research -> AI/SERP observation -> source/citation analysis -> content/entity gap -> evidence improvement -> structured answer improvement -> third-party corroboration -> monitoring and measurement.**

## Required Inputs

Use or request:

- Website/domain.
- Brand/entity name and approved description.
- Priority products/services/topics.
- Target audience.
- Target markets/languages.
- Priority commercial and informational pages.
- Important questions users ask.
- Existing SERP/AI-search observations when available.
- Author/expert information.
- Original research, case studies, statistics, or product facts.
- Structured data and entity information when available.
- Brand mentions and third-party references.
- Analytics/search data.
- Client policy on AI crawlers and content reuse.

If an AI platform cannot be observed directly, do not claim current citation or visibility status without evidence.

## 1. Define the Four Optimization Layers

### AEO - Answer Engine Optimization

Improve content so direct questions can be answered clearly and accurately.

Focus on:

- Direct answers.
- Definitions.
- Step-by-step explanations.
- FAQs when genuinely useful.
- Comparison tables.
- Concise summaries.
- Clear supporting evidence.

### GEO - Generative Engine Optimization

Improve the site's source-worthiness for generative search systems.

Focus on:

- Unique value.
- Original evidence.
- Expert credibility.
- Clear claims.
- Citation-ready facts.
- Entity consistency.
- Third-party corroboration.
- Strong topical context.

### AIO - AI Overview / AI Search Experience Optimization

Analyze queries where AI-generated summaries or answer modules appear and improve the site's eligibility as a supporting source.

Focus on:

- Query intent.
- Source patterns.
- Supporting citations.
- Concise answer sections.
- Strong evidence.
- Freshness where required.
- Organic visibility and CTR impact.

### LLM Optimization

Improve how the brand, people, products, services, and expertise are represented across information sources likely to inform LLM-powered discovery.

Focus on:

- Entity clarity.
- Consistent facts.
- Credible third-party mentions.
- Structured information.
- Authoritative pages.
- Source coverage.
- Factual stability.

Do not promise that any optimization guarantees inclusion in an AI-generated response.

## 2. SEO Foundation Gate

Before AI-specific recommendations, check:

- Important pages are crawlable.
- Important pages are indexable when intended.
- Canonicals are correct.
- The site works on mobile.
- Content matches search intent.
- Main content is accurate and useful.
- Internal links expose important pages.
- Structured data matches visible content.
- Brand/company/author information is clear.

Do not build low-quality AI-specific pages to compensate for weak SEO fundamentals.

## 3. Query and Prompt Research

Build a set of real questions and prompts around:

- Definitions.
- "What is" queries.
- "How to" queries.
- Comparisons.
- Alternatives.
- Best-option queries.
- Use cases.
- Product/service recommendations.
- Troubleshooting.
- Pricing/value questions.
- Local recommendations.
- Industry-specific questions.
- Brand/product-specific questions.

Group prompts by:

- Intent.
- Funnel stage.
- Topic/entity.
- Market/language.
- Commercial importance.

Do not create artificial prompt lists unrelated to real customer needs.

## 4. AI Search Observation

When current AI-search outputs are available, record:

- Query/prompt.
- Platform/search surface.
- Date observed.
- Whether an AI answer appears.
- Whether the brand is mentioned.
- Whether the site is cited or linked.
- Which competing sources are cited.
- What answer themes recur.
- Which facts/sources are used.
- Whether the answer is accurate.
- Whether the answer contains outdated or incorrect brand facts.

Treat observations as point-in-time evidence because AI outputs can change.

## 5. Source and Citation Analysis

For cited competitor/source pages, analyze:

- Page type.
- Domain reputation.
- Topical relevance.
- Author/expert signals.
- Recency.
- Original research/data.
- Specificity of claims.
- Presence of direct answers.
- Tables/lists/processes.
- External citations.
- Entity clarity.
- Internal linking.
- Structured data.
- Third-party corroboration.

Do not copy the wording or structure mechanically. Identify why the source is useful and trustworthy.

## 6. Citation Readiness

A citation-ready passage should be:

- Specific.
- Accurate.
- Verifiable.
- Clearly scoped.
- Easy to understand without excessive surrounding context.
- Supported by evidence where appropriate.
- Stable enough to reference.

Improve citation readiness with:

- Clear definitions.
- Concise factual summaries.
- Methodology notes.
- Named sources.
- Original statistics.
- Case-study outcomes with context.
- Comparison criteria.
- Step-by-step procedures.
- Dates where facts change.

Never fabricate statistics or research for citation value.

## 7. Direct Answer Structure

For important questions:

1. Answer the question directly near the relevant heading.
2. Add necessary context.
3. Explain steps, trade-offs, or exceptions.
4. Support claims with evidence.
5. Link to deeper internal resources where useful.
6. Provide the appropriate next step/CTA without weakening the answer.

Avoid burying the answer under long introductions.

## 8. Definitions and Concept Clarity

For important entities/concepts:

- Define the term clearly.
- Explain who/what it applies to.
- Distinguish it from similar terms.
- Give examples.
- Keep terminology consistent across pages.

Do not create contradictory definitions across the site.

## 9. Comparison Content

For comparisons:

- Define evaluation criteria.
- Use current facts.
- Explain differences clearly.
- Identify who each option suits.
- Include trade-offs.
- Disclose commercial relationships where required.
- Avoid unsupported "best" claims.

Tables can help when they simplify genuine differences.

## 10. FAQ and Question Coverage

Use FAQs when they represent real customer questions.

Good FAQ answers should:

- Answer directly.
- Be factual.
- Avoid duplicating the entire page.
- Link to deeper explanations if needed.

Do not generate dozens of repetitive questions solely to target long-tail queries.

## 11. Original Research and Data

Prioritize assets that can become primary sources:

- Surveys.
- Benchmarks.
- Internal datasets.
- Industry studies.
- Trend reports.
- Case studies.
- Experiments.
- Calculators with documented methodology.

For original data:

- Explain methodology.
- Define sample size/time period.
- State limitations.
- Preserve source pages/URLs.
- Update when data becomes stale.

Do not call marketing claims "research" without a real methodology.

## 12. Case Studies

A useful case study should specify:

- Client/context when disclosure is allowed.
- Starting situation.
- Action taken.
- Time period.
- Measured outcome.
- Measurement method.
- Important caveats.

Avoid implying one client outcome is a guaranteed general result.

## 13. Expert and Author Signals

Where expertise matters, improve:

- Author name.
- Relevant credentials/experience.
- Bio/profile page.
- Expert reviewer.
- Company expertise.
- Contact/about information.
- Links to verifiable professional sources when appropriate.

Never invent credentials, experts, reviewers, or experience.

## 14. Entity Clarity

Maintain consistent information about:

- Organization.
- Brand.
- Founder/executives when relevant.
- Authors/experts.
- Products.
- Services.
- Locations.
- Parent/sub-brands.

Keep consistent:

- Names.
- Descriptions.
- URLs.
- Logos/identity.
- Product naming.
- Location details.
- Relationships among entities.

Entity consistency should extend across the website and trustworthy third-party profiles/mentions.

## 15. Organization and About Information

Ensure users and systems can understand:

- Who the organization is.
- What it does.
- Where it operates.
- How to contact it.
- Who is responsible for content/services.
- Important products/services.
- Relevant credentials/partnerships only when real.

Do not hide important identity information behind vague marketing language.

## 16. Structured Data

Coordinate with technical SEO to use structured data that accurately describes visible entities and content.

Potentially relevant types include:

- Organization.
- Person.
- Product.
- Service where supported by current schema/search guidance.
- Article.
- Breadcrumb.
- LocalBusiness.
- Event.
- Dataset.

Do not add schema for content or claims not visible/real on the page.

Do not treat schema as a guarantee of AI citation.

## 17. Third-Party Corroboration

AI/LLM visibility can benefit from consistent, credible information beyond the brand's own site.

Look for legitimate:

- Editorial mentions.
- Industry publications.
- Research citations.
- Partner pages.
- Professional profiles.
- Directories with real trust/discovery value.
- Podcasts/interviews.
- Conference/event profiles.
- Reviews/marketplace listings where applicable.

Coordinate with off-page/backlink rules. Do not manufacture citations or fake mentions.

## 18. Brand Mention Analysis

Track:

- Brand mentions with links.
- Brand mentions without links.
- Product/service mentions.
- Executive/expert mentions.
- Sentiment/context.
- Factual accuracy.
- Competitor mention share.

Correct inaccurate owned-source information first. Handle third-party correction/outreach professionally.

## 19. Knowledge Consistency

Audit important facts across:

- Homepage.
- About page.
- Product/service pages.
- Author pages.
- Contact/location pages.
- Structured data.
- Social profiles.
- Major business directories.
- Trusted third-party profiles.

Flag contradictions such as different founding dates, product descriptions, locations, leadership names, pricing facts, or service areas.

## 20. AI Crawler and Access Policy

When crawler access matters:

- Identify client policy on AI crawlers/content reuse.
- Separate search crawler access from training/content-use policy where technically relevant.
- Review robots.txt and other available controls carefully.
- Escalate policy decisions to appropriate human owners.

Do not unblock or block AI crawlers automatically to chase visibility.

Do not claim a specific crawler rule guarantees or prevents citations unless supported by current platform documentation.

## 21. AI Overview / AI Search Query Analysis

For queries with AI answer modules, record:

- AI feature presence.
- Organic position.
- Cited sources.
- Source types.
- Answer angle.
- Brand inclusion.
- Click/CTR trend if first-party data is available.
- SERP changes over time.

If organic ranking stays stable but CTR declines, consider whether AI/other SERP features changed click behavior before concluding the page failed.

## 22. AEO Content Pattern

For answer-oriented sections, use structures such as:

```markdown
## [Question]
[Direct 1-3 sentence answer]

### Key points
- Point 1
- Point 2
- Point 3

### Details / exceptions
[Context and evidence]
```

Use only when it improves reader experience.

## 23. GEO Content Pattern

For source-worthy pages, aim to include some combination of:

- Clear thesis/answer.
- Original evidence.
- Named methodology.
- Expert contribution.
- Specific examples.
- Comparisons.
- Useful tables.
- Primary-source citations.
- Updated facts.
- Stable definitions.

Do not inflate content length solely for perceived generative-search optimization.

## 24. LLM Brand Representation

For important brand entities, ensure the public information footprint consistently answers:

- What is the brand?
- What does it offer?
- Who is it for?
- Where does it operate?
- What makes it distinct?
- Who are the key experts/owners where public and relevant?
- What evidence supports major claims?

Avoid vague brand copy that omits factual identity.

## 25. Product and Service Facts

Make important facts explicit and consistent:

- Features.
- Use cases.
- Pricing model where public.
- Availability.
- Geographic coverage.
- Integrations.
- Limitations.
- Eligibility.
- Support options.

Do not let outdated marketing pages conflict with current product facts.

## 26. Freshness

Refresh facts that change, including:

- Pricing.
- Product features.
- Leadership.
- Regulations.
- Statistics.
- Research.
- Availability.
- Market data.

Do not refresh timeless content merely to change a date.

## 27. High-Risk Topics

For health, finance, legal, insurance, safety, or other consequential topics:

- Use strong primary/authoritative sources.
- Show appropriate expert review.
- Avoid unsupported recommendations.
- Identify geography/jurisdiction.
- Keep facts current.
- Require human/expert approval.

AI-search visibility never overrides safety or accuracy.

## 28. AI-Generated Content Governance

If AI drafts content:

- Verify every important factual claim.
- Check for fabricated citations.
- Check for duplicated/generic language.
- Add original insight/evidence.
- Apply brand rules.
- Apply expert review when required.

Do not mass-publish low-value pages intended only to capture AI/search visibility.

## 29. Prompt/Query Coverage Map

Maintain a table such as:

```markdown
| Prompt/Query | Intent | Market | Business Priority | Existing Page | AI Answer Present | Brand Mentioned | Site Cited | Competitor Sources | Gap/Action |
```

Use it to identify repeatable visibility gaps.

## 30. AI Citation/Visibility Metrics

Track carefully when observable:

- Citation presence rate across a fixed prompt set.
- Brand mention presence rate.
- Competitor citation share.
- Source diversity.
- Citation destination pages.
- Accuracy of brand facts in answers.
- Referral traffic from AI platforms when measurable.
- Organic CTR changes on AI-heavy SERPs.
- Branded search demand/mentions as supporting indicators.

Treat prompt-set measurement as directional, not a universal representation of all AI users.

## 31. AI Search Experiment Rules

For experiments:

- Define prompt/query set.
- Record platform and date.
- Capture baseline.
- Make a specific content/entity/evidence change.
- Keep a change log.
- Recheck after a reasonable period.
- Avoid claiming causation from one observation.

AI outputs can vary between runs, users, locations, and system versions.

## 33. Prioritization

Prioritize AI-search work by:

- Business importance of query/topic.
- User demand.
- Existing organic visibility.
- Frequency of AI answer appearance.
- Competitor citation dominance.
- Availability of unique evidence.
- Entity/factual inconsistency severity.
- Effort.
- Risk.
- Confidence.

Do not prioritize obscure prompts only because an AI answer can be influenced.

## 34. Human Approval Gates

Require approval for:

- Major brand/entity fact changes.
- Regulated claims.
- Publishing new original research.
- Public case-study claims.
- Changes to AI crawler policy.
- Large-scale AI-generated publishing.
- Material schema/entity changes with business implications.
- Third-party correction campaigns involving sensitive claims.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Never guarantee AI citations, AI Overview inclusion, LLM recommendations, or rankings.
- Never fabricate citations, research, experts, statistics, reviews, or brand mentions.
- Never create separate low-quality pages only for AI systems.
- Never treat schema as a citation shortcut.
- Never optimize at the expense of factual accuracy.
- Never assume one AI response is stable or representative.
- Never present observed AI output without the observation date/platform when freshness matters.
- Never allow AI-search goals to override legal, safety, brand, or expert-review requirements.
- Never abandon traditional SEO fundamentals when optimizing for AI search.
